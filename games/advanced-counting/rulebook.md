# Advanced Counting Rulebook
(From Patrick's Google Drive — "Advanced Counting Rulebook", last updated Dec. 2022. History only: the build spec in SPEC.md overrides it where they differ.)

Game Designer: Patrick Blim-Hockberger · Creation Date: 2019 · Playtesters: Robert Warmka, Shawn Murdzek · Players: 1-2 · Ages: 10+ · Duration: 10-20 min.

## Overview
How high can you count? Players play individually with their own decks. Their cards are numbered from 1-64. Start by shuffling 1-9 and deal them in a grid. Memorize the locations of the cards. When ready, flip the cards face down and attempt to flip them sequentially from 1-9. Every time you succeed, more cards are added! How high can you count?

## Components
A deck of cards numbered 1-64

## Setup
Each player needs their own deck of cards. Cards need not be ordered perfectly from 1-64, but they should be ordered by letter from A to K. Pick a variant (see below). For your first game you may choose to start with just cards 1-4, then add 5-6, then 7-9.

## Play
Shuffle the level A cards and deal them face up in a 3x3 grid. Attempt to memorize their locations, then place them number-side down and attempt to flip them sequentially starting from 1. Keep trying until you succeed. Each time you succeed, shuffle in the cards of the next letter, which represents the next level, and deal a new grid with an additional row or column, making a square whenever possible. See how high you can count! Whenever you give up, your score is the highest level you successfully completed.

## Variants
- Casual: How high can you count? No timer, no stress, just play. Can you break your record?
- Timed: Set a timer. 10 minutes for a short game, 20 for long. What is the highest level you can complete before the timer is up? When the timer goes off, you may flip all cards face down and make one final attempt.
- Multiplayer: Whomever counts highest wins! Can be played casual or timed. Supports additional players with additional copies of the game.
- Target: Choose a target level to complete. Solitaire, you can time how long it takes to reach that target and try to improve your record time. Multiplayer, the first player to complete the target level wins.
